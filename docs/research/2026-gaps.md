# bobo gaps: missing papers, techniques, phenomena and play styles

*2026-09-30. What bobo's simulations are missing, judged on three things: **simulation novelty**, **authenticity** (what you see, hear and feel comes out of the sim and responds to how you touch it), and **sensory satisfaction**. Game design (goals, levels, failure, progression, charts) is out of scope on purpose. The doc builds on `physics.md`, `2026-tech.md`, `cloth-knitting.md`, `shear-knit.md`, `2026-wave3.md` §1 and `2026-audit.md`, and does not repeat what they already cover (Mixwell, Dripping Thin Films, Fire-X, flow maps, HOME-FREE foam, Stable Cosserat Rods, BladeMaster, VR-Doh, erosion, Snap the Branch and so on). Where one of those is the right answer, it is named in a few words and not re-argued.*

**Legend.** Effort: **S** is under a week, **M** is 1 to 3 weeks, **L** is more. Feasibility is for an iPhone 13 mini (A15) at 60 fps in WKWebView and is **(est)** unless a source says otherwise. DOIs were checked against Crossref on 2026-09-30. Project URLs were opened (HTTP 200). **(unverified)** marks anything I could not confirm.

---

## 0. The main gap: sound is the least simulated layer

bobo's visuals are simulated, but most of its **sound is not**. `src/shell/sound.ts` plays 14 fixed voices and 5 filtered noise loops, and toys only change their pitch and gain. The one physical path is `sound.modes()` (modal synthesis). It builds one `OscillatorNode` per partial (at most 12), is limited to 14 voices shared with everything else, and only Foundry's planned rework uses it. So a brick, a jar and a porcelain shard all ring as the same `knock` played at a different pitch, and water sounds come from a pre-baked `bubble` buffer, not from the bubbles the sim makes.

The research is 20 years old, cheap, and fits a phone, because audio synthesis costs little next to the render. **The biggest authenticity gain per hour of work in bobo is making sound an output of the sims.** Most of the top 10 below comes from this.

## 4. Beloved sandbox toys and interaction styles bobo is missing

### 4.1 Reference sandboxes: what makes their feel special, in simulation terms

| Reference | What the sim does that makes it feel alive | Lesson for bobo |
|---|---|---|
| **The Powder Toy** ([GitHub](https://github.com/The-Powder-Toy/The-Powder-Toy)) | The README says it simulates "air pressure and velocity, heat, gravity and a countless number of interactions between different substances". **Continuous fields layered under the particles** (pressure blasts that push powders, heat that melts and boils) plus electronics (spark propagation). | Alchemy Sandbox is a pure CA. Adding a coarse **pressure/velocity field and a heat field** under it (bobo already has Stable Fluids and heat code) makes explosions push, fires loft ash, and metal conduct. This is the single biggest Alchemy upgrade. |
| **Powder Game** (Dan-Ball) ([page](https://dan-ball.jp/en/javagame/dust/)) | The original: a wind field you "blow" with drags, plus fans and players. | Direct wind painting as a verb. It maps to the mic "blow" in 4.2. |
| **Sandspiel** ([sandspiel.club](https://sandspiel.club/)) | CA plus a WebGL fluid for wind (per `physics.md`), and user-made elements. | Its fluid-under-CA coupling is the model for the Alchemy fields above. |
| **Noita** ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/881100/Noita/); GDC talk in `physics.md`) | *Every pixel is simulated*. Detached pixel islands become rigid bodies that fall and shatter back into pixels. | This is the audit's Dig Down fix. It also suits Foundry's mould break and Alchemy (a stone arch collapsing). |
| **Teardown** ([site](https://teardowngame.com/); dev blog [design notes](https://blog.voxagon.se/2020/11/05/teardown-design-notes.html)) | Began as "a technology experiment" with play designed around the tech. Voxel destruction where structure really fails and fire really spreads through materials. | Supports bobo's sandbox framing: build play around the sim, not the other way round. It also argues for structural failure (connected-component support) in Demolition. |
| **Universe Sandbox** ([site](https://universesandbox.com/)) | "N-body simulation at almost any speed", a material system (hydrogen, iron, rock, water) and temperature, so collisions melt and vaporise. | **Time-scale control** as a verb (scrub sim speed; slow-mo already exists as a juice trick). A phone-scale "Planet Marbles" toy could use N-body plus merge. |
| **Algodoo** ([site](https://www.algodoo.com/)) | 2D rigid bodies, "turn objects into water", lenses and laser beams, gears and motors. | **Optics as a sim** (refracting lasers through bobo's jelly and water) is a satisfying, cheap addition that no bobo toy has. |
| **Little Inferno** ([Tomorrow Corporation](https://tomorrowcorporation.com/littleinferno)) | Every item burns in its own way, with its own sound. Fire is the toy. | Per-material combustion and sound (the §2.3 and 1a fire sound). Campfire should let you burn different *things*, not only logs. |
| **World of Goo** ([site](https://www.worldofgoo.com/)) | Spring structures that sway, sag and snap under load, and the balls chatter. | Load you can feel: sag and creak before failure. It maps to §2.5, 2.1 and 2.4. |
| **Pavel Dobryakov's WebGL Fluid** ([live](https://paveldogreat.github.io/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation/)) | Instant response under the finger, bloom, dye above 1.0. | Already bobo's Ink Swirl. The missing half is **sound from the flow** (Dobashi, 1a). |
| **Townscaper** (Oskar Stålberg) | Not a physics sim (it is constraint-based placement), but every tap gives an immediate, surprising and coherent response. Its web page says "WebGL builds are not supported on mobile devices". | The feel lesson is **zero-latency, always-coherent response**, which bobo already aims for. |

*"Oddly satisfying", slime and kinetic-sand apps:* I could not verify specific titles without search, so I name none **(unverified)**. What sets them apart in simulation terms is usually **sound and haptics synced to deformation** (a slime squelch that follows stretch rate, sand's crunch as it's cut). That is exactly bobo's gap in §0.

### 4.2 Interaction verbs and inputs bobo doesn't use yet

bobo today uses touch drag, tap and flick, multi-touch in Ferro, Peel, Prints and Spool, and tilt through `devicemotion` in Splash, Goo, Pour and Sand Jar. The microphone is never used (`getUserMedia` does not appear in `src`).

| Verb or input | How (web and WKWebView) | Physical coupling it enables | Toys |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blow (mic)** | `getUserMedia` → `AnalyserNode`. Breath is broadband, low-frequency-heavy noise with high spectral flatness, while voice is harmonic, so the two can be told apart. Map RMS to airflow. | A real airflow source for the fluid grids: fan Campfire, blow out a Candle, fog the Frost Window, clear Fingerprint dust, blow bubbles (Bubble Wand), gust the Laundry | Fire, Prints, Laundry, Ink, and new Candle, Frost, Bubble |
| **Sing or hum (mic pitch)** | Autocorrelation pitch tracking on the same analyser | **Resonance**: drive a mode at the matching frequency. Shatter the Singing Glass, form Chladni patterns, raise Faraday waves | new Singing Glass, Chladni, Pool Speaker mode |
| **Rub** (fast small circles) | Detect the circular-motion frequency from pointer history | Stick-slip friction exciter (§2.1) | Singing Glass, Chladni bow, Prints brushing |
| **Shake** | `devicemotion` acceleration, which bobo already reads for tilt | Impulses to the whole scene: rattle magnet balls, emulsify Pour Sort's oil (audit), settle Sand Jar strata, loosen Dig soil | Pour, Sand Jar, Dig, and new Magnet Balls, Hourglass |
| **Flip or turn the phone over** | `deviceorientation`, full rotation | Real gravity reversal: an hourglass (the Beverloo flow, 1b), a snow globe | Sand Jar, and new Hourglass or Snow Globe |
| **Two-finger twist** | Angle between two pointers | Torsion: twist spaghetti before snapping (PNAS 2018), wring laundry, twist bubble wrap, turn the pottery wheel's speed | new Snap!, Bubble Wrap, Laundry |
| **Two-hand pinch and stretch** | Distance between pointers | Stretch goo and taffy (Goo), pull dough, stretch a soap film until it pops | Goo, Jelly, Bubble |
| **Press depth** | iPhone 13 mini has **no 3D Touch**, so `Touch.force` isn't useful on the perf-floor device ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Touch/force)). The substitutes are hold duration and contact radius growth (`radiusX` support on iOS is **unverified**). | Press strength into clay, bubble wrap and ice | Pottery, Bubble Wrap, Frozen Pond |
| **Long-press heat** | Hold duration as a heat source in the shared heat field (§2.10) | Melt a peephole in frost, soften wax, warm cobalt ink so it shows | Frost, Candle, Invisible Ink |

## 5. Top 10 recommendations

Ranked by (simulation novelty × authenticity × satisfaction) ÷ effort. Items 1 to 3 are upgrades to existing toys, and 4 to 10 are new toys, most of which reuse the item 1 infrastructure.

| # | What | Why | Key paper | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Sim-driven contact sound bus** (AudioWorklet modal bank plus event bus), first wired into Demolition, Knockdown and Kintsugi | Turns bobo's biggest non-simulated layer into sim output across a dozen toys. Every block, can and shard sounds like its own size and material, and like how hard it was hit. | van den Doel, Kry & Pai, FoleyAutomatic (2001) [doi](https://doi.org/10.1145/383259.383322) | M |
| 2 | **Bubble voice driven by entrainment** in Splash, Pool rain, Pour and Goo water | Water sounds as big or as small as the splash that made it. Very cheap, and it gives the water toys their missing sense. | van den Doel 2005 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1145/1101530.1101554); Moss et al. 2010 [project](http://gamma.cs.unc.edu/SoundingLiquids/) | S |
| 3 | **Campfire sound from heat release, plus mic blowing** | The roar, gasp and whumpf follow the oxygen field the toy already simulates, and blowing into the phone really feeds the fire. | Chadwick & James, Animating Fire with Sound (2011) [doi](https://doi.org/10.1145/1964921.1964979) | S |
| 4 | **Chladni Plate** (new) | What you hear is literally the mode that arranges the sand, a perfect match of sound and sight. It reuses `sound.modes` and the powder grains. | Gander & Kwok (SIAM Review 2012) [doi](https://doi.org/10.1137/10081931x) | S |
| 5 | **Singing Glass** (new) | Stick-slip friction, a water-dependent pitch, a rippling surface, and a mic-sung resonance that can shatter it. Novel on phones, and entirely emergent. | Mertz et al., stick-slip wineglass (JASA 2025) [doi](https://doi.org/10.1121/10.0037898); French 1983 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.13147) | S–M |
| 6 | **Bubble Wrap** (new) | Each pop's snap depends on the pressure you built. Slow squeezes leak and fast ones pop, and twisting the sheet sets off chains. Peak tactile satisfaction for low effort. | Matyka & Ollila, pressure soft bodies [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0407003) | S |
| 7 | **Snap!** spaghetti and breadsticks (new) | Fracture cascades emerge from the rod dynamics. Twist first and it breaks cleanly in two, and each piece rings at its own pitch. | Audoly & Neukirch (PRL 2005) [doi](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.95.095505); Heisser et al. (PNAS 2018) [doi](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1802831115) | S–M |
| 8 | **Honey Drizzle** (new) | Liquid-rope coiling regimes (coils, meanders, figure-eights) come out of one viscous rod as you raise and move the dipper. No phone toy simulates this. | Bergou et al., Discrete Viscous Threads (2010) [doi](https://doi.org/10.1145/1833349.1778853) | M |
| 9 | **Frost Window** (new, mic breath) | Breath fogs the glass, and frost ferns grow from your finger lines by a real crystal-growth CA. It introduces the mic "blow" verb. | Reiter 2005 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0960-0779(04)00374-1); Witten & Sander 1981 [doi](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevlett.47.1400) | S–M |
| 10 | **Alchemy pressure and heat fields** (upgrade) | Powder Toy-style continuous fields under the CA: blasts push, heat conducts and melts, and ash lofts. It reuses bobo's fluid and heat code. | The Powder Toy ([README](https://github.com/The-Powder-Toy/The-Powder-Toy)); Stam 1999 (already cited) | M |

**Close runners-up:** Magnet Balls (Thomaszewski 2008), Pottery Wheel (Dewaele & Cani 2003), Frozen Pond with singing-ice chirps (Pfaff 2014 for the cracks), the Pool Speaker / Faraday mode (S, nearly free), Goo Lab foam and oobleck (Yue 2015), and the Kintsugi tap test (after item 1).
