# bobo audit: the ten newest toys

*2026-09-30. Five independent reviewers read every source file of the ten newest toys and checked the code against what each toy's references, `tech` line and research spec claim. They also judged novelty against the rest of bobo and the games each toy riffs on. The reviewers were language models reading code; the scores rank what to fix, not what players think.*

## Summary

The physics in most of these toys is decoration on a rules-based game: the outcome is decided by hand-written rules, and several cited papers were not what the code does. None was rated strong.

**What changed after the audit (2026-09-30):**
- References that did not match the code were removed or reworded (DeepFracture on Knockdown; the SIGGRAPH 2026 buoyancy paper and Rayleigh–Taylor on Pour Sort; the claim that Invisible Ink reuses Campfire's burn grid; "XPBD film" on Peel It; "cans roll away"). Toys are told apart by their simulation, not their progression ([game-design.md](game-design.md)), so this pass scores only the simulation, with any meta layer turned off: how novel it is, whether what you see, hear and feel emerges from it and responds to *how* you interact, and how rich that sensory response is. Foundry, Spool Sort and Dig Down were scored after their rebuilds.

**Rebuilds.** Foundry now computes its ring from a 2D plane-stress FEM of the actual casting (shift-invert Lanczos, seams softened, voids removed) and fills bottom-gated moulds under a head; Dig Down turns unsupported ground into traced rigid bodies that fall, shatter and re-bake (the Noita recipe); Spool Sort pulls stranded-colourwork yarns through capstan-friction catches. ## Knockdown

**Claimed.** tech: 'Soft-step rigid bodies · Voronoi shatter · WebGL2'. Refs (index.ts:14-39): Box2D v3 soft step + restitution pass, Box2D 3.1 rolling resistance ('cans roll away'), DeepFracture (impact-dependent fracture pattern), Juice It or Lose It. Spec (2026-wave3.md §4.1): reuse Demolition World, cans as boxes that become circles when tipped, 3 pre-baked Voronoi patterns per jar picked by impact point, jam decals, hit-stop, material-specific sound.

**What the code does.** physics.ts is a near-verbatim copy of src/toys/demolition/physics.ts (its own header at physics.ts:1-4 says so; diff is ropes/grabs removed plus ~60 lines: per-body restitution/oneWay/ghost/gravityScale fields at physics.ts:47-54, a Box2D-v3-style post-solve restitution pass at physics.ts:~609-630, and a one-way-shelf early-out at physics.ts:367). Those additions are real and correct, but they are small. The main thing you do (knocking items off shelves) is not rigid-body physics at all. It is a scripted 2.5D depth rule. The thrown ball is a collision-less ghost while it rises (index.ts:495). At the top of its arc it is hit-tested against item outlines, and the item gets a hand-written 1D momentum exchange (index.ts:917-926, strike() at index.ts:563-588). That fills a fake depth velocity it.vz, which slows at a per-material 'slide' rate (stall.ts:20, index.ts:874-877). Past DEPTH=0.5 the item turns into a ghost and 'falls behind' (index.ts:877, 938-949). Neighbours are shoved by a BFS over bounding-box gaps with a hard-coded 0.7 share (spread(), index.ts:594-614), not by contacts. The 2D solver only handles what falls sideways, the shelves and debris. Glass: a real Voronoi diagram is built by half-plane clipping (shatter.ts:39-58), but each cell is replaced by a shrunken oriented box (shatter.ts:59-87, '*0.8', 'boxes overlap where the cells don't'), and the renderer draws one of 3 generic shard outlines (shape: i%3, shatter.ts:86). The 'impact-dependent' choice is a 3-way switch on the local x of the hit (index.ts:648). All items, cans included, are Shape.Box (index.ts:284). Cans never become circles, so they tip and slide rather than roll. Crates 'splinter' into 3-4 horizontal slabs (index.ts:~717-742). Jam is ballistic particles stamped into a 512² decal (index.ts:694-701, 1066-1100). This is decent juice, but it isn't simulated fluid. Sounds are the shared bobo voices (knock/tick/snap/thud) at different pitches and delays (index.ts:808-845), not synthesis driven by the material.

**Reference issues.** Box2D v3 and 3.1: honest and used, but that work was done in Demolition, and the restitution pass is the only new use. DeepFracture: decorative. There's no learned model and no impact-conditioned pattern generation, just 3 fixed seed sets and a left/centre/right switch. Citing a neural fracture paper for this overstates it. 'Juice it or lose it' is a generic GDC talk on YouTube, fine as inspiration but it isn't a technique. No ref covers the thing that actually decides the gameplay (the scripted z-push and chain spread), and none is needed because it's ad hoc. The spec's claim that 'rolling resistance makes cans roll away' is false in practice: cans are boxes.

**Derivative of.** bobo Demolition Tower (same solver file copied, same cannonball, breakables, shock propagation and sleeping) plus Jelly Merge's hit-stop. On the market it is a direct Smash Fest! clone, and the App Store already has a game literally named 'Carnival Knockdown' ('smash glass jars with a satisfying ASMR'), plus Royal Smash! and Carnival Blast!. The spec itself scores its novelty 3/5 and 'Effort: S', and admits it follows the hit 'almost for free'. The core loop (limited-ammo throws at tin-can pyramids) is decades-old fairground and Flash-game material (Angry Birds and Can Knockdown lineage).

**Weaknesses.** 1) Reskin: the solver is copied and only restitution plus one-way shelves were added. 2) The hero mechanic is faked. 'Knocked off the back of the shelf' is a scripted 1D collision plus a deceleration and a depth threshold, so a hit's result barely depends on the stack's structure, contact geometry or where you hit. The chain reaction is a BFS with a fixed 0.7 share, not physics. 3) 'Voronoi shatter' is Voronoi only in name once cells become shrunken overlapping boxes drawn with 3 stock outlines. Glass doesn't break where or how it was hit beyond a 3-way switch. 4) Cans are boxes, so the tin-can roll, the most characteristic can behaviour, is missing. 5) Material 'ASMR' is pitch-shifted shared samples, while the market leader's selling point is distinct material sound. 6) There is nothing a Smash Fest player hasn't seen, and it has less content (4 layouts, random stacks).

**Proposed fix.** Make the break itself the novel, physical part. Replace the pre-baked boxes with runtime fracture seeded at the actual contact point, using the contact impulse to set seed density. Keep the resulting convex polygons as real polygon bodies (add a polygon-polygon SAT manifold to the World) instead of OBB stand-ins. Then drive each fragment's sound from its impulses with modal synthesis, so a shard's size and material set its pitch. References: Müller, Chentanez and Kim, 'Real Time Dynamic Fracture with Volumetric Approximate Convex Decompositions' (SIGGRAPH 2013) for impact-local Voronoi fracture; van den Doel, Kry and Pai, 'FoleyAutomatic: Physically-based Sound Effects for Interactive Simulation and Animation' (SIGGRAPH 2001) for modal impact sound. Also remove the scripted z-push. Either model the shelf depth for real (a 2-plane stall where items are knocked off the front, in the side view) or let tipping and toppling in the 2D plane clear the shelf, so the outcome depends on where you hit the stack.

## Spool Sort

**Claimed.** tech: 'Mass-spring knit + verlet yarn'. Refs: Knit Out and 'the 2025 yarn-sort wave (Yarn Loop, Wool Crush)' (linked to the pocketgamer.biz homepage), Yuksel 2012 Stitch Meshes ('a picture as a grid of stitches whose connections decide what holds and what falls away'), Jakobsen 2001 (verlet plus distance constraints for knit and yarn), Kaldor 2008 ('feel reference: pulling a yarn tugs the neighbouring stitches before they let go'). Spec (2026-directions.md:42): 'The knit-puzzle wave, but with real yarn physics nobody else has', built on Pull the Thread's knit and yarn.

**What the code does.** The knit is a 2-D verlet grid, one node per stitch, with structural and diagonal-shear distance constraints (index.ts:153-167) solved in 14 Jakobsen iterations (index.ts:582-594). Jakobsen is honestly used. However, every stitch is pulled 6% toward its fixed home position every frame (HOLD, index.ts:44-45, 578-580), and the top row is hard-pinned (index.ts:596-600), so the knit is basically a pinned board that jiggles. The gameplay is pure grid rules. A stitch is 'exposed' only if the stitch below it is gone (index.ts:281-284). A tap does a breadth-first walk over same-colour exposed stitches up to spool capacity (index.ts:287-329). Unravelling one stitch is kinematic: the node is moved toward the spool at a fixed rate and released when a timer reaches 1 (index.ts:420-433), with no tension test. The Kaldor 'tug' is faked with a fixed position kick to the 8 neighbours (index.ts:512-513). The Yuksel 'connections decide what falls' is a 4-neighbour flood fill from the top row (index.ts:519-547). Disconnected chunks fall off screen and count as progress for free (index.ts:603-606). The yarn is a 16-node verlet chain whose segment length is set to 1.06× the endpoint distance each frame (index.ts:642-667): a decorative droop between the stitch and the spool. The spool colour is chosen by the game to match a reachable colour (index.ts:239-255), and unstick() auto-clears jams (index.ts:466-487). Stitches are drawn as two ellipses per 'V' (index.ts:723-768). It does not import or reuse Pull the Thread's XPBD knit, even though the spec says it is built on it.

**Reference issues.** The Knit Out ref points at the pocketgamer.biz homepage, a generic link, and the game names 'Yarn Loop' and 'Wool Crush' are unverified. Yuksel 2012 is decorative: stitch meshes are a 3-D polygonal knitting topology, and nothing here goes beyond a colour grid with 4-neighbour connectivity. Kaldor 2008 is decorative ('feel reference'): no yarn-level contact, sliding or loop interlock, just a fixed neighbour nudge. Both papers are copied from the thread toy's refs (thread/index.ts:16,22), where they are at least closer to the implementation. Jakobsen is accurate.

**Derivative of.** bobo: thread (Pull the Thread) is already a knit that unravels stitch by stitch into yarn, with the same Yuksel and Kaldor refs and stronger physics (XPBD cloth plus an XPBD strand, where stitches pop when the strand is pulled taut, thread/sim.ts:1-3, 479-530). Spool is a weaker reimplementation with a colour-sort layer. cloth and laundry already cover the verlet/XPBD grid. Market: this is the 2025 yarn/wool colour-sort genre (knit picture plus bottom-up exposure plus tap-a-spool-to-collect plus limited rack slots), close to pixel-flow and bus-jam-style sorters. The mechanic is copied almost directly, and the rack is less of a puzzle because the game picks spools for you and auto-unjams.

**Weaknesses.** The physics has no effect on the outcome. Exposure, reel order, release and chunk-drop are all grid rules or timers, and the verlet knit is pinned to its home positions, so it is visual jiggle. The 'real yarn physics nobody else has' pitch is not delivered: the yarn is a cosmetic droop rope with a length reset every frame. The puzzle has no failure and few real decisions, since pickColor feeds reachable colours and unstick() removes jams, so the game mostly plays itself. It is a regression from the existing thread toy's XPBD tension-driven unravel, and it reuses that toy's paper refs decoratively. The design reference is a homepage URL. Falling chunks give free progress, which undercuts the 'stitch by stitch' fantasy.

**Proposed fix.** Make yarn tension decide the puzzle by moving it onto Pull the Thread's XPBD knit with real knitting topology. In intarsia or fair-isle, each colour is its own continuous yarn running along the course, and fair-isle 'floats' carry unused colours behind other stitches. Reeling a spool puts XPBD tension on that colour's strand. A stitch releases only when local tension exceeds a threshold based on how many live neighbour loops still interlock it. A strand that runs under a still-knitted float of another colour snags and drags that patch along, or jams. Order then matters for physical reasons rather than an 'exposed below' rule, and the market genre has nothing like it. Grounding: Kaldor et al. 2008 for loop-interlock behaviour; Cirio et al., 'Yarn-Level Cloth Simulation with Sliding Persistent Contacts' (TVCG 2017) for cheap yarn-over-yarn sliding (citation believed correct, verify); Leaf et al., 'Interactive Design of Periodic Yarn-Level Cloth Patterns' (SIGGRAPH Asia 2018) for per-stitch yarn paths (uncertain applicability). Also remove the auto-unstick and the free-progress chunk drops so choices carry weight.

## Dig Down

**Claimed.** tech: 'Cohesive falling-sand CA'. Refs: A Game About Digging a Hole (Steam page, design), Noita GDC 2019 ('falling-sand cells updated bottom-up, with rigid ground that crumbles into loose particles'), Sandspiel ('per-material CA rules; each soil adds a cohesion rule for overhangs'). Spec (2026-directions.md:43): 'soil holds until undercut, then crumbles; built on Sand CA (cohesive soil) + Demolition rigid bodies'.

**What the code does.** A small standalone Sandspiel-style CA of its own (src/toys/dig/sim.ts). It shares no code with sand/sandjar. Materials are one byte per cell, and a LOOSE flag bit (sim.ts:16) is the only difference between 'packed' and 'grain'. Terrain is layered bands from three summed sines plus elliptical blobs (sim.ts:52-90). Loose grains fall straight or diagonally with a 55% refusal for non-sand to steepen piles (sim.ts:169-195). That is the whole 'angle of repose'; there is no friction or pile-slope test. Water does down, diagonal, then 2-cell sideways hops (sim.ts:197-228). There is no per-tick moved stamp, so on a left-to-right sweep water can be processed twice in one step. The 'cohesion' is a 1-D row scan: an undercut packed cell looks up to SPAN[m] cells left and right for a neighbour that is solid and has solid below it (sim.ts:142-150), then crumbles with a hand-tuned per-material chance (sim.ts:19,24,153), plus a 45% chance of taking a side neighbour with it (sim.ts:158-163). The scan never checks that the cells in between are filled, so support gets counted across a gap. The comment at sim.ts:149 describes gap handling that does not exist. ROCK has CRUMBLE=0 and SPAN=99 (sim.ts:19,24), so fully undercut boulders float forever. Nothing uses the Demolition rigid bodies the spec promised. Finds are emoji drawn behind the grid (index.ts:331-337) that 'pop' when an openness test goes over 55% (index.ts:273-280). They never fall, roll or get buried by a cave-in. Chips are cosmetic ballistic particles (index.ts:225-252). The sim only runs the visible rows ±30 (index.ts:169).

**Reference issues.** Noita is cited for 'rigid ground that crumbles', but Noita's signature tech is pixel rigid bodies (marching-squares outlines fed to Box2D) that break into particles. None of that is implemented here, only a flag flip. Sandspiel is a generic falling-sand homepage, and the 'cohesion rule' is this toy's own heuristic, not something from Sandspiel. The Steam store page is a design ref, which is fine, but the market game is 3D voxel digging with upgrades, so the resemblance is thematic. No ref backs the cohesion or span model, and none exists: it is ad hoc.

**Derivative of.** bobo: sand (Alchemy Sandbox) and sandjar already cover falling-sand CA with the same two refs (Noita, Sandspiel). This is a third, simpler CA with a digging brush and a camera. rinse and sandjar already cover the reveal-and-progress loop. Market: 2-D destructible dirt with falling grains and buried collectibles is old and common: Mr. Driller, Dig Dug, Terraria/Worms-style terrain, 'Dig This!', 'Deep Town' and many hyper-casual 'dig for treasure' ads. The shelf of emoji finds is a standard collection meta.

**Weaknesses.** The one differentiator, cohesive soil, is a crude row-distance heuristic with random crumble chances, not a support or stress model, and it counts support across gaps. Boulders hover in mid-air when undercut, which breaks the physical read in the rock band. The rigid bodies the spec promised were dropped, so there are no slabs toppling, boulders rolling or finds tumbling. Finds are static sprites, so the physics never affects the reward. There is no per-tick moved stamp, so water can double-move. Terrain generation is layered sine bands, so every backyard looks alike. It is borderline 'ok', because cave-ins are physics-driven and do give the toy its moments, but it is thin, and a sand-CA reskin next to Alchemy and Sand Jar.

**Proposed fix.** Replace the SPAN heuristic with real structural islands in the style of Noita (Purho, GDC 2019). Each frame after a dig, run connected-component labelling (union-find) over packed cells. Any island that no longer connects to bedrock or the side walls becomes a rigid body: trace its outline with marching squares and simulate it with Demolition's existing soft-step solver. On impact it shatters into LOOSE grains above an impulse threshold. Finds and boulders become rigid bodies too, so they drop, roll and get buried. Undercutting then produces real slab collapses and toppling boulders, which drive both the fun and the reward. A heavier alternative is 2-D MLS-MPM (Hu et al., SIGGRAPH 2018) with Drucker-Prager plasticity plus a cohesion term (Klár et al., SIGGRAPH 2016); phone performance at this grid size is uncertain.

## Kintsugi

**Claimed.** tech string: 'Voronoi fracture + 2.5D tumbling shards + 1D seam flow'. Refs: Kintsugi (Wikipedia), Voronoi diagram (Wikipedia), Diamond-square/midpoint displacement (Wikipedia). Spec (2026-theme-toys.md 3.3): break using demolition/physics.ts breakable rigid bodies, a joint/position constraint to snap, and gold as 1D flow along crack polylines with speed from slope and viscosity, with Dripping Thin Films named as the upgrade path.

**What the code does.** Fracture (fracture.ts:93-194): real half-plane-clipped Voronoi cells in a unit box. Seeds use best-candidate sampling biased toward a random 'impact' point (101-118), up to 24 retries reject slivers (120-126), and shared edges get recursive midpoint displacement (jag, 58-72). The cells are then warped onto the silhouette (toLocal/sh.map). This is precomputed before the drop, and the impact point has nothing to do with how the bowl lands. It is sound, but it is the same impact-seeded Voronoi idea already in knockdown/shatter.ts:1-4. The drop (index.ts:448-451) is a scripted z-fall. Shatter (633-656) gives each shard a radial velocity plus random spin. 'Tumbling' (677-744) is a fake z-hop with bounce damping, exponential friction, a hard push off the mending area (724-730) and a disc-overlap separation between shards (746-764). There is no rigid-body contact or polygon collision, and none of demolition/physics.ts is used, even though the spec says to use it. Reassembly (555-576): the dragged shard lerps toward the finger with a quadratic pull to its home (561-562). Its rotation decays to 0 automatically while held (567-568), it seats when within 20px and 0.3 rad (571-575), and release within about 70px also seats it (582). The shard's exact home outline is drawn highlighted while held (938-947). So there is no fitting puzzle: orientation and position are solved for you. Gold (gold.ts): each seam segment carries a fill fraction from each end. Speed = clamp(0.6±0.9·dirY, 0.3, 1.5)·speed/len (166-167), and a full segment wets both end nodes, which floods adjacent open segments (wetNode 129-137). This is a rate-limited graph flood fill from an infinite source. Nothing is conserved: no volume, no capillarity, no pooling. 'Viscosity' is just a divisor on speed (index.ts:800), and pressure only scales it by 0.7-1.0. The glinty gold is Canvas stroke layering with a moving gradient (985-1044).

**Reference issues.** All three refs are generic Wikipedia pages. Voronoi and midpoint displacement are really used (fracture.ts:130-144, 58-72), so they are honest but not research. The Kintsugi page is theme decoration. The tech string's '2.5D tumbling shards' oversells a z-hop sprite with disc repulsion. '1D seam flow' is really a BFS-style front propagation with a slope-clamped speed and no flow physics (no mass, no viscosity model, no capillary law). The spec's Dripping Thin Films upgrade and demolition breakable bodies were not implemented and are not cited, which is correct, but the viscosity slider implies a physical model that doesn't exist.

**Derivative of.** Within bobo: the fracture is knockdown/shatter.ts's impact-seeded Voronoi idea again (without the rigid-body world knockdown puts the shards into). Pour/hold-to-fill with a % ring echoes rinse, pour and the progress-reveal pattern. Market: a drag-to-home jigsaw with ghost outlines and magnetic snap, as in countless 'restore/repair' ASMR puzzle ads, plus pottery-repair sequences in Assemble with Care (ustwo, 2019) and several App Store 'Kintsugi' relaxation puzzles (titles uncertain). Hold-to-fill-a-line is the same interaction as line-fill/'fill the path' hypercasuals.

**Weaknesses.** (1) There is no puzzle. Rotation auto-corrects (index.ts:568), a magnet pulls shards home (561), the exact target outline is highlighted (939-947) and release seats from about 70px (582), so any drag in the right direction wins. (2) The break is cosmetic. The pattern is precomputed, the impact point is random rather than from the fall, and shards don't collide as polygons, so the drop has no physical consequence. (3) The gold is a flood fill with an infinite source. Where you pour, how much, and how the bowl sits make no real difference. The slope term only changes speed within 0.3-1.5x, and 'viscosity' is a speed scalar, so the core fantasy (gold running through cracks) is scripted. (4) Spec reuse of demolition physics was dropped. (5) Every ref is Wikipedia. (6) The visual quality (procedural glaze painter, pieces.ts) is where the effort went, not the simulation.

**Proposed fix.** Make the gold a conserved, gravity- and capillary-driven flow that the player has to steer. Give the crucible a finite volume. Model each crack segment as a capillary channel whose wicking follows Lucas–Washburn (L² ≈ γ r cosθ t / 2μ) plus a gravity-drainage term along the segment's slope in world space. Let the player tilt the mended bowl (gyro or two-finger rotate) so gold pools at low junctions, runs dry uphill, and drips off rim edges if overfilled. A clean, full, even vein then becomes a skill result, not an inevitability. As a secondary fix, remove the home highlight and rotation auto-correct and snap shards to neighbours by matching jagged edges, the standard fragment-reassembly approach (e.g. Papaioannou et al., 'Virtual Archaeologist: assembling the past', IEEE CG&A 2001; citation uncertain). This would make fitting a real puzzle.

## Pour Sort

**Claimed.** tech string 'Clavet particle stream · density layers · WebGL2'. Refs: Clavet 2005 double-density relaxation (stream necks/drips); SIGGRAPH 2026 buoyancy-driven phase separation MPM (light layer rises through heavy); Rayleigh-Taylor instability (why the lighter layer climbs as blobs); Sutherland-Hodgman (exact tilted-tube volume); Helmholtz resonance (pour sound brightens as tube fills). Spec (2026-wave3.md §4.3) pitches 'Nobody pours real liquid', flow rate follows tilt, pour stops when colour runs out or B is full, drag-to-pour by hand, Tube Maps for SPH wall density.

**What the code does.** The game is a stock Water Sort discrete puzzle: tubes are int stacks (logic.ts:16-20), canPour/applyPour (logic.ts:44-72), a best-first solver (logic.ts:102-137) and a seeded generator (logic.ts:223-259). The whole move is decided instantly on tap in startPour (index.ts:469-474, applyPour before any liquid moves). The Clavet stream is real but purely cosmetic: stream.ts:150-198 does genuine double-density relaxation (rho/rhoN, P and PN, pairwise displacement), with a clamped negative-pressure hack for cohesion (stream.ts:176) and a linear-only, clamped viscosity impulse (stream.ts:265-296; Clavet's quadratic beta term and the viscoelastic springs are dropped). Nothing the particles do can change the outcome: drops that land in the wrong tube are deleted (stream.ts:241, 256), a stall fallback forces the flow when the tilt geometry does not spill (index.ts:663-665), and stepFinalize overwrites the target's layers with the exact precomputed stack (index.ts:762-763). 'Oil floats/syrup sinks' is Array.sort by density (logic.ts:57-62). The 'rise' is a scripted smoothstep volume crossfade between two layer entries (index.ts:789-803), and the blobs are 6 sine-positioned circles (index.ts:921-927). No MPM, no buoyancy, and no Rayleigh-Taylor dynamics. Sutherland-Hodgman is real and used correctly: half-plane clip plus shoelace and bisection for level/spill angle (geom.ts:9-35, 82-117), which drives the tilt animation and the flow rate (index.ts:656-662). Slosh is two hand-tuned damped oscillators (index.ts:625-630). 'Helmholtz' is a linear map from fill to a bandpass brightness param (index.ts:849). Drag-to-pour by hand from the spec is not implemented (input is tap-only, index.ts:421-423). Tube Maps is not used; walls are clamped boxes plus a circle (stream.ts:212-263).

**Reference issues.** Buoyancy 2026 (MPM phase separation) is decorative: the code is a stable sort plus a crossfade. Linking a WebGPU MPM paper implies physics that isn't there. The URL is a conference-schedule page and hasn't been verified. Rayleigh-Taylor (Wikipedia) is decorative and generic: the blobs are sin() placements, not an instability. Helmholtz (Wikipedia) is wrong physics as well as generic: an open tube's air column is a quarter-wave pipe resonator, not a Helmholtz resonator, and the code is just a linear brightness ramp. Clavet 2005 is real and the DOI looks right, but only the double-density half is used (no springs, no quadratic viscosity), and it has no effect on gameplay. Sutherland-Hodgman is honest but cited via Wikipedia. The spec's Tube Maps ref was dropped from the code, which is fair, but the spec still claims it.

**Derivative of.** The Water Sort / Magic Sort / Liquid Sort genre (identical rules plus a density exception; oil/syrup 'special liquid' variants already exist in some sort clones [unverified]). Inside bobo it ports Ferro Blob's Clavet core (stream.ts:3 says so; the constants are copied from ferro/sim.ts:187-245) and Splash Tank's devicemotion path. It overlaps thematically with Sand Jar and Spool Sort (pour/sort into containers by colour).

**Weaknesses.** The toy's premise is 'real liquid pouring', but the liquid is a particle animation layered over a discrete puzzle that has already resolved. The cohesion slider, tilt and stream shape never change a result, so the physics doesn't drive the fun. The only new rule, density reordering, is a one-line sort that also makes the puzzle easier: different densities can always stack, so the move space grows. The 'rise through' spectacle is keyframed. Two of five refs are decorative Wikipedia links and one is the wrong acoustic model. Drag-to-pour, the one spec feature where the player would control the flow, is missing. Under the shader polish it is a Water Sort clone.

**Proposed fix.** Make the liquid the game state instead of a garnish. Simulate each tube's contents as a small 2D multiphase SPH/PBF body: Solenthaler and Pajarola 2008, 'Density Contrast SPH Interfaces' (SCA 2008), or Macklin and Mueller 2013 'Position Based Fluids' with per-phase rest density. Simulate only the source and target tubes at full rate, and freeze solved tubes to a layer stack. Then make volume continuous and let the player hold-and-tilt to pour, as the spec wanted. The amount transferred is whatever particles actually cross the lip. Overpouring spills or tops up the wrong colour. Violent pours or phone shakes emulsify oil into droplets that separate again over time by real buoyancy, so Rayleigh-Taylor-like plumes emerge instead of being keyframed. Puzzles then become about pour control and settling time ('wait for the oil to rise before you pour the water off the bottom'), which no Water Sort game has. For the sound, replace 'Helmholtz' with a quarter-wave resonance f = c/(4·L_air) on the rising air column.

## Mow Lines

**Claimed.** tech string 'Shell-textured grass + cut map · WebGL2'. Refs: Lengyel et al. 2001 shells (lawn quad drawn 16x); Acerola fur video (hashed blade cells, taper test, discard, fake AO); Daniel Ilett grass map (value = blade length, mowing writes into it); Grass Master (SayGames) as a design reference. Spec (2026-wave3.md §4.4) also promises lean-map stripes from travel direction, curl-noise wind, dirty-rect texSubImage2D uploads, cheaper rendering as the lawn is cut, load-driven motor bog, and a *pattern* mode (match a checkerboard or Bobo face, scored by stripe direction, 1-3 stars).

**What the code does.** The shell texturing is genuinely implemented: one instanced draw of N shells over a 24x38 grid (index.ts:909-910), with top shell first for early-z (shaders.ts:48). Each shell discards outside a rotated, staggered hashed blade cell with a taper radius (shaders.ts:95-109), plus length-map discard (shaders.ts:87) and height-based darkening (shaders.ts:112-115). The CPU R8 length map and RG8 lean map (lawn.ts:100-102) get a swept-capsule stamp per frame (lawn.ts:175-217). Length is min(len, cut), and lean is overwritten with the unit heading, a hard binary write with no bending dynamics (lawn.ts:206-209). Only the dirty rect is uploaded (index.ts:720-737). Stripe shading is dot(lean, away-from-camera) (shaders.ts:128-131). Wind is the analytic curl of a sum-of-sines stream function, evaluated per vertex (shaders.ts:38-56), which honestly matches the 'curl' claim. % mown is a CPU counter (lawn.ts:201-204). The load-driven motor/haptics are a mapping of removed length/s onto loop level and brightness (index.ts:524-525, 819-842). The mower is a speed-capped lerp-chase of the finger (index.ts:587-595), the stone is a circle push-out plus a timed stall (index.ts:604-627), the cat is a teleport-target walk (index.ts:674-718), and clippings are ballistic points with drag (index.ts:786-807). Regrowth steps +1 per 1.5 s over the whole map and marks everything dirty (lawn.ts:220-236, index.ts:527-532). Pattern mode, the one real goal beyond coverage, does not exist: there are no 'pattern' or 'checker' matches in src/toys/mow. The win condition is just 98% coverage (index.ts:540-548).

**Reference issues.** All four refs map to code that exists. Lengyel 2001 (hhoppe.com) is the right primary source, though the lawn is a flat quad, so the 'arbitrary surfaces' part isn't exercised. The Acerola YouTube ID is not verified. Ilett is a blog tutorial, a generic secondary source for 'a texture holds grass length'. The Grass Master App Store page is a market reference, not tech, and is flagged as such. Nothing is decorative, but nothing here is research either: a shell-fur tutorial plus a paint mask.

**Derivative of.** Grass Master – Cutting Game (SayGames) and Lawn Mowing Simulator: same verb, same stripes, same coverage-% reveal. Inside bobo it reuses Power Rinse's 'brush a mask to ~98% with a progress meter and celebrate' loop (rinse/index.ts:450-475), swapping dirt for grass length. The shell/cut-map layer is new code for bobo, but the idea is copied straight from shear-knit.md §3.1/§7.2 and standard shell-grass tutorials.

**Weaknesses.** The core loop is scratch-card coverage: drag until the counter hits 98%. It adds no mechanic over Grass Master or Rinse. The only planned differentiator, pattern mode scored by stripe direction, was cut. Stripes come from a binary overwrite of heading, so stripe quality involves no skill (speed, overlap and double-cuts don't matter), and the grass has no physical response: nothing bends under the deck, springs back, clumps or gets flattened by wheels. The mower, stone and cat are scripted rules, not physics. The rendering tech is solid and honestly cited, but it is a well-known shell-fur recipe; the physics doesn't drive the fun, the reveal does. Global regrowth re-uploads the full map every 1.5 s, which is a mild inefficiency.

**Proposed fix.** Turn the lawn into a physical, directional canvas and ship pattern mode. Replace the binary lean overwrite with a per-texel damped bend field: each texel stores a bend vector and stiffness, the deck and wheels apply an impulse and flatten it, and blades relax back with a spring whose rest state depends on how many times and how fast that texel was pushed. Wheel ruts then press harder than the deck, a second pass in the same direction deepens the stripe, and a sloppy diagonal crossing muddies it. This is in the spirit of GPU responsive grass bend/recovery maps, e.g. Orthmann, Rezk-Salama and Kolb 2009 'GPU-based Responsive Grass' (uncertain citation), or Ghost of Tsushima's procedural grass (Wohllaib, GDC 2021). Then score pattern mode by comparing the bend-direction field against a target vector image, a checkerboard or a portrait drawn in lawn stripes. The skill becomes planning paths to paint an image in light and dark, which Grass Master doesn't do and which reuses the same map Shear needs.

## Invisible Ink

**Claimed.** tech: 'CPU heat + scorch grid · WebGL2 composite'. Refs: Wikipedia Invisible ink (lemon juice browns before paper; cobalt ink needs more heat), Wikipedia Heat equation (explicit five-point diffusion), Noita GDC talk (per-cell fire that spreads and burns material away, 'as in Campfire's Burn grid'). Spec (§3.2) says it is built on Campfire's Burn (fire/burn.ts), reveals like Power Rinse's mask, and uses Campfire's flame and oxygen look.

**What the code does.** sim.ts is a new, standalone Paper class. It does not import or reuse Burn (fire/burn.ts), and it has none of Burn's oxygen field. Implemented: (1) An explicit 5-point Laplacian with a linear source and a Newton cooling term, sim.ts:139-141 (honest, matches the Heat equation ref). It runs one step per render frame with variable dt and no substeps (index.ts:319). With diffuse=5 the explicit stability bound D*dt<=0.25 breaks at dt>0.05 s (a 20 fps hitch). (2) Ink development is linear-above-threshold and irreversible: devA += (T-thA)*k*dt and devB likewise (sim.ts:176-177). (3) Browning, char and ignition use one scalar 'dose' that accumulates linearly above thScorch (sim.ts:159), with IGNITE=1.6. There is no Arrhenius or time-temperature kinetics, just thresholds. (4) Fire spread passes a strength value that decays by 0.88 per hop and dies below 0.28 (sim.ts:143-170). It is a hand-tuned rule that makes holes 'gutter out'. There is no fuel or oxygen, and burning cells are clamped to T>=1.35 (sim.ts:149). (5) The flame is an analytic anisotropic Gaussian heat stamp (sim.ts:107-123). The draught is a sum of sinusoids (index.ts:383-385), so 'wind' is scripted, not simulated, and the paper doesn't affect the flame at all. (6) Words are graded by box-averaging the mask (sim.ts:208-223, index.ts:408-427). Decode and loss are threshold checks (index.ts:435-446). (7) The first 6.5 s are a scripted auto-sway ad demo (index.ts:355-365). (8) Most of the visual richness is in the shader (shaders.ts:140-241): noise-cut ragged hole edges, blackbody ember rim, flame tongues from a 7-tap vertical smear of the fire channel. The ragged burn edge is fbm noise on a thresholded field (shaders.ts:166), not a simulated front. The sheet tilt is cosmetic (index.ts:374).

**Reference issues.** Heat equation (Wikipedia): accurate and actually used, but it is a generic textbook link. Invisible ink (Wikipedia): partly misused. Cobalt(II) chloride ink is famously reversible (it turns blue when heated as it dehydrates and fades again as it reabsorbs moisture), but devB here is permanent (sim.ts:177), so the one distinctive property of the second ink is missing. Lemon-juice 'browning' is modelled as a lower linear threshold, not as a chemical reaction rate. Noita GDC talk: decorative. Nothing specific from it is implemented, and the 'use' text claims it is 'as in Campfire's Burn grid', but Burn isn't used (no import). The spec's 'built on Burn' and 'Campfire's oxygen' claims are false for the shipped code.

**Derivative of.** bobo: the reveal-a-hidden-mask-and-report-a-fraction loop is Power Rinse (rinse) with heat instead of water. The fire and char look overlaps Campfire (fire). The 'keep moving or it overheats' meter is the same push-your-luck tension as many existing toys. Market: reveal-under-a-mask is the scratch-card or 'wipe to reveal' hypercasual staple. Burning-paper and hidden-message ASMR toys are common (Little Inferno for burn-as-toy; plenty of 'secret message' reveal puzzles in escape-room games). The theme is well matched to the physics, but the mechanic is familiar.

**Weaknesses.** The skill is one-dimensional: move the sheet so no cell's dose crosses a threshold. Nothing is emergent, because char is a monotone function of time-above-threshold at each cell. The 'second ink' only differs by threshold, so it adds no new verb. The draught is sinusoidal scripting, not an air field, so 'harder letters' are just a heavier random wobble. The fire has no oxygen or fuel, and the gutter-out behaviour is a strength-decay constant. There is an explicit-diffusion stability risk on frame hitches (no substeps or clamp). The spec's claimed reuse of Burn is not true, so the tech line oversells what is implemented. A lot of the perceived quality is shader dressing (noise-cut holes, fake flame tongues) over a simple thresholded scalar field.

**Proposed fix.** Replace the linear 'dose' thresholds with a real smouldering-combustion front. Add an oxygen or airflow field advected by the draught and by a buoyant plume above the flame (reuse and extend Burn's oxygen grid, which the spec promised). Use Arrhenius-rate reactions: ink development with a low activation energy, cellulose pyrolysis and char with a high one. Then burn fronts show the fingering instability of Zik, Olami and Moses, 'Fingering Instability in Combustion', PRL 81, 3868 (1998). Char eats in oxygen-starved, branching fingers that depend on which way the air flows. 'Low and slow' versus 'hot and fast' becomes a real trade-off that the Arrhenius rates create, and holding the sheet upwind versus downwind becomes a skill. Also make the cobalt ink reversible with a moisture field, so it fades back unless read while warm. That gives the second ink a genuinely different verb.

## Foundry

**Claimed.** tech: 'Falling-sand CA with molten metal cells + temperature'. Refs: Noita GDC talk (bottom-up falling-sand, liquids and crumbling solids in one grid), Sandspiel (per-material CA rules, temperature-dependent metal flow), Wikipedia Sand casting (flask/basin/sprue/cavity, shake-out), Wikipedia Casting defect (misruns and cold shuts set the ring). Spec (2026-theme-toys.md §3.1) also promises: viscosity rising as metal cools, Campfire's heat ramp and glow, an optional hand-off of chunks to demolition/physics.ts for the smash, and chime pitch and clarity driven by fill quality.

**What the code does.** sim.ts is Dig Down's grid pattern (packed cells, LOOSE=0x80, shade, crumbled counter, alternating scan direction: compare dig/sim.ts:16,35) with a METAL cell that carries a Float32 temperature. Things that are real: (1) bottom-up scan with alternating direction, sim.ts:151-153 (Noita-style, correct). (2) Per-cell cooling: 4-neighbour metal averaging (sim.ts:215, a relaxation step, not a conservative flux), a constant heat sink per touching sand cell (sim.ts:216), and air cooling only from the open top (sim.ts:207-221). Sand has no temperature of its own. It never heats up or saturates, and 'scorch' is only a colour counter (sim.ts:212). (3) 'Viscosity' is a movement probability of 0.25+0.75*fl plus a sideways reach of 1-4 cells (sim.ts:240,253). That is a stochastic rule, not viscosity. (4) The cold shut is a flag heuristic: a cell that just came to rest next to a SKIN-flagged neighbour at least 0.1 colder gets SHUT (sim.ts:274-284). A design hack clamps the air-cooled top at FREEZE+0.05 while pouring so the sprue can't plug (sim.ts:219-221). That removes true misruns from the top surface. (5) Quality = (filled - 8*seamCells)/cavity (sim.ts:347). (6) The best part is the crucible: it pivots about its lip, and the pour rate comes from polygon-clipped area below the lip line (crucible.ts:24-58, index.ts:429-437), so tipping further pours faster. (7) Break-out loosens a disc of sand to LOOSE (sim.ts:128-141) and flips a random crumble flag (sim.ts:169-177). There is no demolition hand-off. The casting is a sprite that falls with a hard-coded 260 cells/s² gravity and a damped spring on its angle (index.ts:595-621). (8) The 'ring' is sample playback: the shape's pitch is a constant per shape (shapes.ts:31,52,70,87; shapePitch index.ts:1302) times 0.8+0.2q, and clarity = smoothstep(q) picks between two canned chime and thud patterns (index.ts:644-663). Where the voids and seams sit, and the casting's real geometry, have no effect on the sound. Only the scalar q does. (9) The heat colour ramp is its own ramp (index.ts:93-126), not Campfire's. There is no pressure or metallostatic head: shapes.ts:2-3 admits the liquid can only fill downward, so every mould is designed top-fed and upside-down to hide this.

**Reference issues.** Noita GDC talk: generic inspiration. The bottom-up update is used, but that technique is in every falling-sand sim (and already in dig and sand). Sandspiel: a homepage link with no specific technique, and Sandspiel has no temperature-dependent metal viscosity to borrow. Both Wikipedia links are generic background and describe the theme, not the method. The Casting defect page's physics (fronts that fail to fuse because of oxide film and temperature) is reduced to a one-bit flag heuristic. The spec's Campfire glow reuse and demolition physics hand-off did not happen. The thermal model and the ring have no references at all, and the ring is the payoff the whole toy is sold on.

**Derivative of.** bobo: close to Dig Down (dig/sim.ts): same cell encoding, LOOSE flag, crumble counters and hammer-loosen mechanic, plus a temperature channel. The pour-and-fill-to-a-line loop overlaps Pour (pour) and Sand Jar (fill a shape with falling grains, then reveal). Market: molten-metal pour-into-mould videos are a well-worn hypercasual and ASMR trope (e.g. 'Blade Forge 3D' or 'Sword Maker'-style casting games and 'metal casting' ASMR apps; exact titles uncertain). Breaking the mould to reveal the item is the familiar surprise-reveal beat. Novel here: cooling that grows a skin, so hesitating mid-pour punishes you, and grading by sound.

**Weaknesses.** The payoff (ringing the casting) is fake. It maps one scalar onto two canned sample patterns, so a bell with a hole in the lip sounds the same as one with a hole in the crown. There is no pressure field, so metal cannot rise and moulds are designed around the limitation. The thermal model is not conservative: sand is an infinite sink, and neighbour averaging doesn't transfer heat properly. The cold-shut rule is a hand-tuned flag with a clamp (FREEZE+0.05) that stops real misruns during the pour, so the 'bad pour' state is mostly scripted by the rule rather than emerging. Break-out is random crumble plus a spring-animated sprite, not fracture. Skill ceiling is thin: hold steady at a rate, and the optimal play is always 'hold continuously'. Most of the 1415-line index.ts is rendering, bloom, particles and phase scripting rather than simulation.

**Proposed fix.** Make the ring real. Build a mass-spring or FEM lattice from the actual cast cell mask. Give sound cells full stiffness, reduce the stiffness of SHUT/seam cells, and remove void cells. Solve for the lowest 3-5 eigenmodes (Lanczos or a few power iterations on a sub-1000-node graph run in well under a frame at shelf time) and synthesize the note as a sum of damped sinusoids at those frequencies. This is modal sound synthesis as in O'Brien, Shen and Gatchalian, 'Synthesizing Sounds from Rigid-Body Simulations' (SCA 2002), and van den Doel and Pai, 'The Sounds of Physical Shapes' (Presence 1998). Then a lap line across a bell's waist audibly splits or beats the partials, each casting's timbre is unique, and the grade comes from physics instead of a smoothstep. Second priority: add a hydrostatic pressure solve on connected metal regions (as in the Noita/Powder Toy liquid-pressure approaches, citation uncertain) so metal can fill upward through risers and gating becomes a real design choice.

## Peel It

**Claimed.** tech: 'XPBD film + Kendall peel · WebGL2'. Refs (index.ts:13-38): Kendall 1975 (F = G·w/(1−cos θ)), XPBD (Macklin 2016) for film, curl hinges and a soft glue layer, Small Steps (Macklin 2019) with 10 substeps, Ten Minute Physics #14 grid cloth. Spec (2026-wave3.md §4.2): reuse Tear Sheet's Cloth and tear(), Laundry break-at-stretch pins as adhesion, Kendall-scaled break stretch, a curl bending constraint with 'Better Bending' (SIGGRAPH 2026) as the reference, strain whitening, and a see-through blurred film.

**What the code does.** A new, purpose-built film sim (sim.ts, 835 lines), not a copy of cloth/sim.ts. It's a grid sheet with alternating diagonals (sim.ts:249-268). Edges use a PBD distance projection with a 1e-9 'compliance' and no accumulated lambda, so they are effectively plain PBD, not XPBD (sim.ts:429, 457). There are 10 substeps x 2 iterations (sim.ts:121-122, 369-377), which fits Small Steps. Curl is a 3-vertex hinge whose middle vertex is pushed to a set offset from the chord along the normal, with an XPBD-style alpha (sim.ts:479-508). It's a simple curvature constraint, not a Kirchhoff-Love/'Better Bending' energy. Adhesion is a zero-length positional pull: each substep a glued vertex keeps 50% of its displacement from rest ('give', sim.ts:549-551). That's a lerp, not an XPBD constraint. Kendall is really used, in its inextensible form. updatePeelAngles() (sim.ts:561-611) computes a per-piece factor 1/((1−cos θ)(1−side·s²)), clamped to [0.5, 12], from the peel front's outward direction versus the hand. kendall(i) (sim.ts:625-661) computes a local factor by walking up to 7 cells along the peeled arm, capped at 6. A glued vertex releases when its displacement from rest exceeds stick·K·rateMul (sim.ts:536). If stick·K·rateMul > tough, the piece tears at the front instead (sim.ts:533-535, tearAt 673-681), which is a real peel-versus-tear competition. A made-up viscoelastic rate term, 1+1.7·max(0, v−1)^1.5 (sim.ts:520), makes fast yanks grip harder. Crack tips weaken aligned neighbour edges so rips run (sim.ts:699-710), and union-find tracks the pieces (sim.ts:776-821). The hand height is fixed at LIFT=0.42 (index.ts:45, 383), so the peel angle comes only from in-plane drag. The renderer (render.ts:1-7, 83, 125-132) does see-through film with a 7-tap blur and haze, an environment streak along the curl, strain whitening and a projected shadow. That matches the spec and is well done.

**Reference issues.** Kendall 1975 is genuinely used and drives the gameplay, but only the inextensible term. The paper's title is 'the elastic term', and the elastic term (F/b)²/(2Eh) is exactly what the code omits. The threshold is on glue displacement per substep (a force proxy), not on energy release rate. The DOI was flagged [unverified] in the spec. It looks right (J. Phys. D 8, 1449), but the sources don't confirm it. XPBD (Macklin 2016) is overstated. Film edges are plain PBD with a negligible compliance and no lambda, and the glue is a 50% position lerp. Only the curl hinge uses a compliance term. Small Steps is honest. TMP #14 links to the Ten Minute Physics index page, not the episode (generic). The spec cites 'Better Bending (SIGGRAPH 2026)' for the curl, but the code doesn't implement any of it (it's not in the toy's refs, so it's the spec, not the toy, that is decorative). The rate-dependent 'viscoelastic adhesive' is real physics (e.g. Gent and Petrich 1969 on rate-dependent peel strength, a citation I'm unsure of) but is uncited, and here it's an invented curve.

**Derivative of.** Loosely bobo's Tear Sheet (cloth: XPBD grid, tearing, crack propagation) and Laundry (break-at-stretch pins), in concept. The code is new, and the reveal render is new. On the market, peeling a screen protector or sticker is a common ASMR verb (Mystery Dumpling's unwrap, many 'peel/satisfying' hyper-casual apps, and screen-protector peel levels in ASMR compilations). Those are almost always a scripted wipe mask or a flip animation. Making peel-versus-tear depend on the pull angle is not something the spec found in any competitor.

**Weaknesses.** 1) The physics claim is somewhat inflated: 'XPBD film' is mostly PBD plus a position lerp for the glue, and the curl is a chord-offset hack. 2) Kendall's elastic term is dropped and the threshold works on displacement, so film stiffness and width don't enter. It's 1/(1−cos θ) as a multiplier on a tuning constant, with several invented factors on top (side shear 0.8, kCap 6 vs kMax 12, rate^1.5). 3) Hand height is fixed at 0.42 (index.ts:383), so the player can't lift toward themselves. The angle is set only by where you drag relative to the front, which weakens the headline 'slow and high' promise. 4) With defaults (stick 0.03, tough 0.3), tearing needs K·rateMul > 10, meaning a peel angle below about 26° at slow speed. In practice speed, through the invented rate curve, probably decides tearing more than angle (plausible, not measured). 5) The crackle sound and haptics are driven by release counts, not by a stick-slip mechanism, so the 'zip' texture is a mapping, not something that emerges. 6) Four hand-authored scenes, and the reveal is the only payoff.

**Proposed fix.** Replace the displacement threshold with a cohesive-zone adhesive layer: a per-vertex traction-separation law with a damage variable, fracture energy G that depends on velocity (with the negative-slope region real tapes have), and Kendall's full elastic term using the film's stretch stiffness. Peel speed, angle and film stiffness would then set the peel force together. Stick-slip 'zipping' (the rhythmic crackle, and bright/dull stripes on the adhesive) would emerge from the sim and could drive the audio and haptics directly, instead of counting releases. References: Kendall 1975 (full relation including the elastic term); Barenblatt/Dugdale-style cohesive zone models as used in graphics fracture (e.g. Pfaff et al., 'Adaptive Tearing and Cracking of Thin Sheets', SIGGRAPH 2014, uncertain fit); and the stick-slip peeling physics of Cortet, Ciccotti, Vanel and Santucci, 'Imaging the stick-slip peeling of an adhesive tape under a constant load' (J. Stat. Mech. 2007, citation uncertain). Also let the hand height follow the gesture (for example, pull-toward-camera from drag speed or a second finger) so the 'high angle' is something the player actually chooses.

## Fingerprint Dust

**Claimed.** tech string: 'Powder grid + Gabor ridges + XPBD tape'. Refs: Cappelli et al. SFinGe (ICPR 2000) for iterated oriented Gabor filtering of noise; Sherlock & Monro 1993 zero-pole orientation model; Kendall 1975 peel via Peel It's film. Spec (2026-theme-toys.md 3.4): powder as falling-sand grains (from sand/sandjar) over a hidden adhesion mask, blowing to clear loose grains, and lifting with Peel It's tape.

**What the code does.** Fingerprint synthesis (fingerprint.ts) is real. The orientation field is θ = rot + ½Σarg(z−core) − ½Σarg(z−delta), the Sherlock–Monro zero-pole model (59-71; arches use an ad hoc atan hump, 63-66). This is followed by 9 iterations of convolving noise with 24 quantised oriented Gabor kernels plus tanh soft-clipping (78-99, 127-148), a simplified SFinGe with no frequency map or density variation. It does produce ridge endings and bifurcations. Powder (powder.ts) is two scalar fields per cell (loose, stuck) plus the mask, not falling-sand grains. lay() splits deposited powder by grip = min(1, 5m²) (137-145). brush() presses loose into stuck on high-mask cells, advects loose 2 cells along the stroke, and scrubs stuck off low-grip cells (153-201). blow() strips weakly held stuck powder and advects loose radially, removing 38% (204-239). Airborne grains are a damped point-particle system that deposit via lay() on landing (271-309). This is a heuristic but coherent adhesion model, and it drives the reveal (reveal metric, 314-322). Tape: it really reuses peel/sim.ts's XPBD film with its 1/(1−cos θ) Kendall release factor (peel/sim.ts:4, 536, 606-656), configured at index.ts:511-513. However, the powder the tape carries is a snapshot copied when the tape is laid (index.ts:518-535). Release only clears the grid under released vertices (583-590), and the evidence card paints the whole snapshot regardless of tears or peel quality (paintEvidence 464-495). The peel physics therefore doesn't affect the outcome. Powder choice (black/silver/magnetic) only changes colour and sparkle (54-58); 'magnetic' has no distinct behaviour. Matching (673-701) is a tap on one of 3 cards. On the first case the decoys use different pattern classes (267-268), so it is solvable at a glance. Later cases add one same-class decoy.

**Reference issues.** SFinGe and Sherlock–Monro are specific, correct and actually implemented (fingerprint.ts:59-71, 78-148), which is better than most bobo refs. Caveats: SFinGe is simplified (no ridge-frequency map, no seed-point initialisation, arch orientation not from the zero-pole model), and the 2000 ICPR DOI is plausible but I did not verify it. Kendall 1975 is honest only by inheritance: it lives in peel/sim.ts, and in this toy the peel's outcome is thrown away because the lifted image is a pre-taken snapshot. The spec's 'falling-sand grains' claim is not what was built (it is a density grid, not a CA), but the tech string correctly says 'Powder grid'. No ref backs the powder adhesion model, which is fine because it is not presented as research.

**Derivative of.** Within bobo: the tape lift is a direct import of Peel It (src/toys/peel/sim.ts, render CAM_D, same pick/makeGrab handle code, index.ts:954-1014). The hidden-image reveal-by-rubbing mechanic is a sibling of rinse (erode dirt to reveal) and ink-letter (heat to reveal hidden writing), so it is the third 'rub a mask until the secret appears' toy. Market: dust-for-prints minigames are a staple of Criminal Case, CSI: Hidden Crimes and many hidden-object detective games, and scratch-card reveal is a hypercasual genre. The spec itself rated it the most familiar of the four ('Fresh 1 of 3').

**Weaknesses.** (1) The peel is decorative. Because the tape snapshots the powder when laid (index.ts:518-535) and the evidence ignores tears and peel speed (464-495), the one piece of real mechanics carried over from Peel It has no effect on the result. (2) Matching is a 3-card visual pick. Case 1 decoys differ in pattern class, so you can solve it without dusting well. There is no minutiae-level comparison even though the generator produces minutiae. (3) Powder types are recolours; magnetic powder (a real, different technique: a magnetic wand with no bristle contact) behaves identically. (4) The reveal is a mask-gated scratch card at heart. The powder model is a nice heuristic, but the ridges come out wherever you rub, with no over-brushing penalty beyond haze. (5) It is the third mask-reveal toy after rinse and ink-letter. (6) The spec's falling-sand powder was quietly replaced by a density grid. That's fine, but the spec is now inaccurate.

**Proposed fix.** Make the lifted evidence a product of the peel and the matching a real forensic comparison. At each vertex's release, transfer the stuck and loose powder to the tape with a ratio that depends on local peel rate and angle (rate-dependent adhesion, Gent & Petrich 1969, 'Adhesion of viscoelastic materials to rigid substrates', Proc. R. Soc. A; citation uncertain). Slow, high-angle peels then lift crisp ridges, yanks smear or leave powder behind, and tears lose regions. Then run a real minutiae pipeline on the lifted image: thin the ridges and use the crossing number to find endings and bifurcations (as in NIST NBIS MINDTCT), and match it to the suspects with a Bozorth3-style pair-table score. The player marks matching minutiae, and a bad lift genuinely can't reach the 12-point threshold against a same-class decoy. Brushing technique and peel physics would then decide the case.
