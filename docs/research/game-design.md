# bobo and game design

*2026-10-01. This replaces the earlier stance that game design (goals, levels, progression) is out of scope for bobo. The docs that said so now point here.*

## The rule

**Toys must be different from each other in what they simulate and how that feels, not in what you unlock.** Each toy earns its place with a simulation that is new to bobo, and with sight, sound and touch that come out of that simulation and respond to how you play. If you take away a toy's goals and rewards, it should still be fun to play for a minute, and it should still feel unlike the other toys.

Game design is allowed. It sits on top of that toy and cannot replace it:

- **Progression is a layer, not the reason to exist.** Goals, scores, collections, upgrades and idle loops can make a good toy last longer. They can't make a reskinned toy feel new. Two toys whose only difference is their meta game count as one toy.
- **The sandbox always comes first.** A toy opens straight into play. Meta systems (levels, an economy, idle mode) are opt-in, or start only after the first free play. Nothing that is fun to touch sits behind a lock.
- **Upgrades change the simulation, not just a number.** A better tool should feel different under the finger: wider, sharper, cleaner, louder. "+10% coins" is fine as seasoning, but it should never be the only upgrade.
- **Automation shows the sim working.** If a toy can play itself, the automation runs through the same simulation, so it is still worth watching and hearing. Hand play keeps its own rewards.
- **No dark patterns.** No real money and no ads tied to rewards. Odds are shown and outcomes are honest. A near-miss pays something, nothing punishes you for leaving, and offline gains are capped and generous.

## How the audit applies this

The re-judged audit (`2026-audit.md`) scores **Novelty, Emergent and Satisfying** on the simulation alone, and that doesn't change. A toy with a meta layer is scored with the meta turned off. The meta layer is reviewed separately on one question: does it lead you back to the simulation (more varied materials, tools and situations), or away from it (menus, waiting, tapping numbers)?

The examples in the repo already show the pattern. Jelly Merge's combo ladder and Peel It's sticker shelf are light layers over toys that would satisfy without them. Mow Lines shows the failure: its loop is coverage until 98%, so the simulation doesn't carry the fun.
