# bobo wave 3: research refresh and picks

*2026-09-29. A short follow-up to [2026-tech.md](2026-tech.md), [2026-scene.md](2026-scene.md) and [2026-directions.md](2026-directions.md). Waves 1 and 2 have shipped (Marbling Tray, Jelly Merge, Power Rinse, Sand Jar, Spool Sort, Dig Down), so bobo now has 18 toys. This pass looks for what changed from mid to late 2026, re-scores the parked list and the knitting roadmap, and picks wave 3.*

**Markers.** **(src)** means the figure comes from a linked page I fetched. **(est)** is my own estimate, not measured. **[unverified]** means I saw a claim but could not confirm it from a primary source. Web search was unavailable for most of this pass. Discovery came from the SIGGRAPH 2026 schedule site, the SCA 2026 program file, the arXiv API, the GitHub API, the App Store lookup API, YouTube RSS and a few fetched trade articles.

**Short answer.** Wave 3 = **Knockdown**, **Peel It**, **Pour Sort** and **Mow Lines**. All four reuse existing bobo code, run on WebGL2, have a built-in payoff, and fit the two strongest 2026 signals: the breakout of physics-destruction puzzles (Smash Fest) and the continued lead of sort, screw and ASMR.

### 1.4 Demos, engines and channels

- **Browser simulation code is abundant now.** Since June 2026 there are many small WebGPU XPBD cloth and "jelly lab" repos (e.g. [pudding-lab](https://github.com/Aimer779/pudding-lab), [soft-matter-jelly-lab](https://github.com/fab34/soft-matter-jelly-lab)). Squishy jelly remains the default web-toy aesthetic, which bobo already covers.

- **[SNOWFLOW](https://github.com/Noniv/snowflow_demo)** (Jul 2026, about 580 stars): WebGPU/Babylon procedural snow with a deformation buffer and "snow-surf" trails. It's desktop-only by design, but the deformation-buffer idea is the same as a cut map (see Mow Lines).
- **three.js r185–r186** (Jul–Sep 2026) added a TSL Gaussian-splat renderer, SunLight, OIT and a Sculptor addon. There are no new physics examples since `webgpu_volume_fire`.
- **Two Minute Papers**: every upload from Aug 7 to Sep 24, 2026 is AI/LLM news ([RSS](https://www.youtube.com/feeds/videos.xml?channel_id=UCbfYPyITQ-7l4upoX8nvctg)). No new simulation episodes since 2026-tech.md.
- **Ten Minute Physics**: the index still stops at #25, joints ([index](https://matthias-research.github.io/pages/tenMinutePhysics/index.html)). Müller's latest pushes are `tet-cut` (Aug 2026, [repo](https://github.com/matthias-research/tet-cut)) and a game repo. A cutting episode is likely but **[unverified]**.

**Takeaway.** Nothing in late 2026 changes bobo's tech risk. The useful new pieces are rules and tricks:
- buoyant layer ordering (phase separation);
- side labels for cutting (BladeMaster);
- verified bending energies for film curl;
- stochastic erosion.

The picks can stay on proven CPU and WebGL2 code.

## 4. Wave 3 picks and build specs

Common rules for all four:
- Use `ctx.progress(fraction, label)` from the first frame, and `ctx.celebrate(msg)` exactly once per level or run.
- Haptics go through `ctx.haptic('light'|'medium'|'heavy')`, which is throttled to 40 ms. Tie every buzz to a visible event.
- Sound goes through `ctx.sound.play(voice, {pitch, gain, pan})` and `ctx.sound.loop(kind).set(level, brightness)` every frame.
- Budgets are for the iPhone 13 mini at 60 fps (est): CPU physics ≤ 4 ms, GPU ≤ 6 ms, and zero allocations per frame.

### 4.1 Knockdown ★ (the Smash Fest toy)

**Why:** It follows the biggest tactile hit of 2026 almost for free. Demolition Tower already has the solver, the breakables and the cannonball. What's new is **materials that each look, move and sound different**, plus a **clear-the-shelf** goal. That is exactly the recipe FoxData credits for Smash Fest's success.

- **Core interaction:** a fairground stall with 2–3 shelves of stacked items. Drag back from the launcher at the bottom (a slingshot with a dotted arc preview), then release to fire. Each stall allows 3–5 shots. Knock every item off the shelves. Tap Reset for a new random stall; there's no fail screen, you just re-rack.
- **Materials** (a table: density, friction, restitution, break impulse, voice):
  - **tin can**: light and bouncy, rolls on its side;
  - **glass jar**: shatters above an impulse, spilling jam droplets;
  - **wooden crate**: heavy, and splinters into 3–4 planks;
  - **jelly cup** (optional): wobbles, using Jelly Stack's look only.
- **Physics:**
  - Reuse `src/toys/demolition/physics.ts` `World`: Box2D v3-style soft step, rolling resistance, shock propagation, island sleeping, `breakable`/`age`.
  - Cans are boxes, with circles when tipped on their side. Add restitution per material.
  - **Glass shatter:** 3 pre-baked Voronoi patterns per jar size, chosen by impact point (the DeepFracture idea without the network: [project](https://nikoloside.graphics/deepfracture/)). Spawn the shards as small boxes with 1.5 s of life before they fade.
  - **Jam:** 20–40 ballistic droplets. Each one stamps a splat decal into a 512² backdrop texture when it lands.
  - **Hit-stop:** 60 ms, plus a slow-mo of 0.35x for 0.6 s on the final item (copy Jelly Merge's `hitstop`).
- **Rendering:** WebGL2 with Demolition's `render.ts` instanced quads plus a material atlas (can label, glass with a rim highlight, wood grain). Add one decal texture for jam. Draw everything in 1–2 draw calls.
- **Progress / payoff:**
  - `ctx.progress(knockedOff / total, 'cleared')` updates as items cross below the shelf line.
  - On 100%: `ctx.celebrate(ballsLeft ? 'Clean sweep! +' + ballsLeft : 'Cleared!')`.
  - Out of balls: show a "so close" count and one free bonus ball (near-miss tension, no fail state).
- **Sound:**
  - can hit: `knock` at pitch 1.6–2.0 plus `tick` ×2 (a ring);
  - can on the floor: `tick` rolls;
  - glass break: `snap` (pitch 1.4) + `crumble` (pitch 2.0, gain 0.5) + a soft `chime`;
  - jam: `splash` (gain 0.4);
  - crate: `thud` + `knock` (pitch 0.7);
  - launch: `whoosh`; final item: `chime`.
  - Scale gain by impulse and pan by x.
- **Haptics:** `light` on launch; each impact above a threshold gives `light`/`medium` by impulse; glass break gives `heavy`; celebrate gives heavy (built in).
- **Perf budget:** ≤ 60 items + ≤ 150 shards/planks active, 4 substeps, about 2–3 ms CPU (Demolition already runs more bodies). Decal stamps go through `texSubImage2D` of a 32² region per droplet.
- **Effort:** S. An MVP with 3 hand-made stalls is a few hours; tuning the materials is the rest of the day.

### 4.2 Peel It ★

**Why:** Peeling is the purest one-gesture reveal: a slow pull, a crackle, and a glossy surface underneath. Peeling a wrapper is the hook in 2026's *Mystery Dumpling*. Nobody simulates the film, and bobo already has everything it needs: cloth, breakable pins and tearing.

- **Core interaction:** grab the lifted corner tab and drag.
  - The film lifts along a **peel front** and curls back over your finger.
  - A slow, steady pull at a high angle releases cleanly. Yanking at a low angle, or sideways, **tears** the film and leaves a stuck scrap you have to pick at.
  - Scenes:
    1. screen protector on a phone drawn inside the phone (the meta joke);
    2. a sticker off its backing sheet, which then goes onto a sticker shelf;
    3. masking tape off a fresh paint stripe (a crisp edge reveal);
    4. clear film off a shiny gadget.
- **Physics:**
  - Tear Sheet's XPBD `Cloth` (`src/toys/cloth/sim.ts`, with `tear()`), about 24×40 = 960 vertices, 10 substeps.
  - Every vertex also gets a zero-length **adhesion constraint** to its rest spot on the surface. It is the same kind of constraint as Laundry's `Kind.Pin` break-at-stretch (`src/toys/laundry/sim.ts`).
  - The break threshold follows the Kendall peel relation *F = G·w / (1 − cos θ)*: the energy per unit width to peel drops as the peel angle θ rises ([Kendall 1975, J. Phys. D](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/0022-3727/8/13/005); page is bot-walled, DOI from memory **[unverified]**).
  - In code, scale each vertex's break stretch by `1/(1 − cos θ_local)`, clamped. θ_local comes from the lifted neighbour's height and distance.
  - For curl, add a bending constraint with a slightly curved rest angle. [Better Bending (SIGGRAPH 2026)](https://s2026.conference-schedule.org/?post_type=page&p=15&id=papers_1148&sess=sess102) is the reference if the hinge looks wrong.
  - Tearing: when triangle strain exceeds the film's limit, call `tear()` (already tuned to run across the pull).
- **Rendering:**
  - WebGL2, reusing `cloth/render.ts`. The film is a translucent layer whose tint depends on height, with a specular streak and strain whitening (brighter where stretched).
  - The lifted underside shows a faint adhesive sheen.
  - Under the film, the reveal texture is sharper and more saturated than the "through-the-film" view, which is a slightly blurred and hazed copy.
- **Progress / payoff:**
  - `ctx.progress(released / adhesions, 'peeled')`.
  - On 100% with no tear: `ctx.celebrate('One clean peel!')`. With a tear: `celebrate('Peeled!')` and a smaller confetti burst, using the message only.
  - The sticker scene keeps the sticker on a shelf (a localStorage list, as a per-viewer convenience).
- **Sound:**
  - a `crackle` loop, level ∝ adhesion releases per second and brightness ∝ peel speed (the sticky "tsss-k-k-k");
  - `tick` at pitch 2–3 for each cluster of 8 releases;
  - `rip` on tear;
  - `whoosh` + `chime` when the film comes free.
- **Haptics:** `light` every ~6 releases (the throttle keeps it ≤ 25/s), `medium` on tear, `heavy` via celebrate. Later, the native continuous-haptics bridge (README roadmap) can drive a buzz whose intensity follows the release rate.
- **Perf budget:** about 1k vertices × 10 substeps, about 1 ms CPU; one draw for the film plus one full-screen quad.
- **Effort:** S–M. The adhesion + Kendall threshold + render is a few hours. The four scenes and tuning the tear feel take a day.

### 4.3 Pour Sort ★ (liquid sort with real liquid)

**Why:** Liquid sort is one of the biggest casual genres (*Magic Sort!* about 660k US ratings), and "pour material into containers by colour" is 2026's recurring hit shape (Colony Flow, Sandy Jam, Crystal Drop). **Nobody pours real liquid.** bobo's twist comes from SIGGRAPH 2026's [buoyancy-driven phase separation](https://s2026.conference-schedule.org/?post_type=page&p=15&id=papers_363&sess=sess108): liquids have densities, so a light oil poured under syrup **rises through it**. That gives the genre a new, physical rule.

- **Core interaction:**
  - Tap tube A, then tube B. A lifts, tilts over B and pours. The flow rate follows the tilt, and the pour stops when A's top colour runs out or B is full.
  - Drag A to pour by hand if you prefer, and tilt the phone to slosh every tube.
  - Goal: each tube holds one colour.
  - Levels start with 4 tubes × 3 colours. Later levels add "oil" (floats) and "syrup" (sinks) layers that re-order after a pour.
- **Physics:**
  - **Logic:** each tube is a stack of layers (colour, volume, density). A pour moves volume from A's top layer. After the pour, a stable sort by density animates the layers: a lighter layer rises as a column of blobs over 0.5–1 s.
  - **Visual at rest:** each tube's surface is two damped spring modes (slosh), driven by tube motion and `deviceorientation` tilt (Splash Tank's tilt input path).
  - **Visual while pouring:** spawn particles at A's lip, using the **Clavet double-density relaxation** core ported from Ferro Blob (`src/toys/ferro/sim.ts`; [Clavet et al. 2005](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1073368.1073400)). They are cohesive, so the stream necks and drips.
    - Particles collide with B's walls using an SDF capsule; [Tube Maps](https://s2026.conference-schedule.org/?post_type=page&p=15&id=papers_657&sess=sess103) is the reference for cheap curved-wall density.
    - They also collide with B's current surface height, which is treated as a floor.
    - After 0.3 s of rest, a particle is **absorbed**: its volume goes into B's top layer and the particle is recycled.
    - So only the stream is simulated. Cap: 700 particles.
- **Rendering:**
  - WebGL2.
  - Tubes: one shader per tube draws layers from the stack, the meniscus, the slosh surface and glass (rim highlight, a fake refraction offset of the background).
  - Stream: particles splat into a 256² density texture (Ferro's thickness-grid idea), thresholded with a soft edge and tinted by particle colour.
  - Mixing inside B's top 10% is a short blend that settles to the layer's colour.
- **Progress / payoff:**
  - `ctx.progress(solvedTubes / colours, 'sorted')`.
  - Each tube that becomes single-colour and full gets a cork pop and a shimmer.
  - All sorted: `ctx.celebrate('Sorted!')` and the next level.
  - Include an undo (the whole level is a small state).
- **Sound:**
  - a `trickle` loop, level ∝ flow rate. Its brightness **rises as B fills**, since a filling bottle's resonance rises as the air column shortens ([Helmholtz resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmholtz_resonance)). This is the signature cue.
  - `plop` when the stream first hits liquid;
  - `bubble` loop while a light layer rises through a heavy one;
  - `tick` at pitch 3 for glass clinks;
  - `pop` + `chime` for a solved tube.
- **Haptics:** `light` pulses at about 8 Hz while pouring, scaled by flow; `medium` on a solved tube; `heavy` on celebrate.
- **Perf budget:** ≤ 700 particles, 2 substeps, spatial hash, about 1.5 ms CPU. Tube shaders plus the density splat come to about 2 ms GPU. Idle tubes cost only their shader.
- **Effort:** M. The layer logic, tube shader and pour arc are a few hours. The particle stream plus absorb is another half day. Levels with oil and syrup come after that.

### 4.4 Mow Lines ★ (lawn striping, and the stepping stone to Shear)

**Why:** Mowing is 2026's cutting-ASMR verb (SayGames' *Grass Master*, Apr 2026). Clean diagonal lawn stripes are a classic "oddly satisfying" image. The strategic reason matters more: Mow Lines uses **exactly the tech Shear needs**, namely shell fur, a length/cut map, and clipper-style motor sound and haptics driven by load (shear-knit.md §3.1, §5.2, §7.2). It needs a flat lawn instead of an animated sheep, so it de-risks the L-sized Shear for an S–M cost.

- **Core interaction:**
  - Drag a push-mower across a lawn seen in 3/4 perspective. Grass under the deck is cut short, and clippings spray out of the side chute.
  - The **stripes come from your path**: each pass leans the stubble in your direction of travel, so neighbouring passes read light and dark, like a real lawn.
  - Obstacles: flower beds (mowing them loses the "perfect" bonus), a stone that clanks and stalls the motor, and a sleeping cat that moves away.
  - Modes: *free mow*, and *pattern* (match a faint target such as a checkerboard or a Bobo face, scored by stripe direction).
- **Tech:**
  - **Shell texturing** ([Lengyel et al. 2001](https://hhoppe.com/proj/fur/)): 16 opaque shells with discard over a ground quad. Each blade is a hashed cell in UV with a taper test and fake AO by shell height, following Acerola's recipe as summarised in shear-knit.md §3.1.
  - **Length map**: R8 at 256².
  - **Lean map**: RG8 at 256², the direction the stubble was pushed.
  - Every frame, CPU `Uint8Array` mirrors get the deck's capsule stamped in (length = min(length, cutHeight); lean = mower heading), and only the dirty rect is uploaded with `texSubImage2D`.
  - Stripe shading: a light/dark shift from dot(lean, view direction), plus a sheen.
  - Uncut grass sways with a curl-noise wind offset.
  - Optional slow regrowth so free mode never ends.
- **Rendering:**
  - WebGL2, one instanced draw of 16 shells, at DPR 1.5 on the lawn.
  - Cut texels (length < 0.15) discard after shell 2, so a mown lawn gets **cheaper** as you go.
  - Clippings: ≤ 300 instanced quads with drag.
- **Progress / payoff:**
  - `ctx.progress(cutTexels / lawnTexels, 'mown')`, counted on the CPU mirror, so no GPU readback.
  - At ≥ 98%: `ctx.celebrate(flowersHit ? 'Mown!' : 'Perfect stripes!')`.
  - Pattern mode scores stripe accuracy with 1–3 stars.
- **Sound:**
  - a `rumble` loop for the motor. Idle is low. **Load** (texels cut per frame) raises the level and drops the brightness, so the motor audibly "bogs down" in long grass (shear-knit.md §5.2's clipper-bog, done with the existing loop);
  - a `hiss` loop for the clipping spray, ∝ cut rate;
  - `tick` grains at pitch 1.5–2.5 for clippings hitting the chute;
  - `knock` + a motor stall for the stone;
  - `chime` on complete.
- **Haptics:** `light` at a rate ∝ load (throttled, about every 80 ms in thick grass and none in cut grass; the absence of buzz is itself feedback); `medium` on the stone; `heavy` on celebrate. This is the first toy to exercise the continuous Core Haptics bridge from the roadmap, and it has a fallback.
- **Perf budget:** GPU 3–4 ms for 16 shells at DPR 1.5 with discard. Fall back to 12 shells if the frame goes over 14 ms. CPU < 0.5 ms (a stamp of about 30×30 texels per frame plus the counter).
- **Effort:** S–M. The shell shader plus the length map is a few hours. Lean stripes, clippings, sound and the pattern mode take another day.

### Build order

1. **Knockdown**: the fastest ship and the highest trend fit. It also proves out a per-material sound table that other toys can reuse.
2. **Peel It**: small, reuses cloth, and adds a new ASMR verb.
3. **Mow Lines**: builds the shell and cut-map layer that Shear will reuse.
4. **Pour Sort**: the largest of the four; do it last so the others ship on time.

**Alternates:** if Knockdown feels too close to Demolition Tower, swap in **Screw Loose**. It is also S effort, uses the same `World` with revolute joints, and has the twist-to-unscrew `tick` detents already sketched in 2026-scene.md §6.7.
